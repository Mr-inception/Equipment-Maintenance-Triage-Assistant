title: Centrifugal Pump Maintenance Manual

## [PUMP-1.1] High bearing temperature
Normal bearing housing temperature is 40 to 70 degC. Warning above 85 degC. Critical above 95 degC: stop the pump and inspect. Common causes: low, degraded or contaminated lubricant, shaft misalignment, over-greased bearing, excessive axial thrust from running far from the best efficiency point. Inspection: check oil level and colour, check coupling alignment, check for hot spots on the bearing housing, listen for bearing noise, review recent lubrication records.

## [PUMP-1.2] Excessive vibration
Overall vibration velocity is acceptable up to 4.5 mm/s RMS. Alert above 7.1 mm/s. Danger above 11.2 mm/s. Common causes: shaft misalignment, worn bearings, impeller imbalance or damage, cavitation, loose foundation bolts, pipe strain. Inspection: check foundation and baseplate bolts, check coupling alignment, check pipe supports for strain, listen for gravel-like noise that indicates cavitation, check impeller condition at next opportunity.

## [PUMP-1.3] Cavitation (crackling or gravel noise)
Cavitation sounds like gravel or crackling inside the casing and is often accompanied by vibration and unstable discharge pressure. Suction pressure below 0.5 bar gauge is a warning sign. Common causes: blocked suction strainer, partly closed suction valve, low tank level, air leak in the suction line, process fluid too hot. Inspection: check the suction strainer, confirm suction valve is fully open, check tank level, inspect suction line joints for air leaks.

## [PUMP-1.4] Low discharge pressure or low flow
Discharge pressure more than 15 percent below the rated value is abnormal. Common causes: worn wear rings, damaged or clogged impeller, air ingress, wrong motor rotation after rewiring, blocked suction, partly closed discharge valve. Inspection: confirm rotation direction, check both valves, check suction strainer, compare against the pump curve.

## [PUMP-1.5] Mechanical seal leakage
A mechanical seal may weep a few drops per minute at most. Continuous leakage is abnormal. Common causes: dry running, shaft misalignment, worn seal faces, overheating, excessive shaft deflection, abrasive particles in the fluid. Inspection: observe leak rate, check for recent dry-run events, check alignment, plan seal replacement if leakage persists.

## [PUMP-2.1] Safe inspection and lockout
Before any hands-on inspection, isolate and lock out the motor, close suction and discharge valves, depressurise the casing and let hot parts cool. Restart after maintenance requires technician sign-off. This system never starts, stops or controls equipment remotely.
